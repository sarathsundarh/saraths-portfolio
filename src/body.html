Drigil

2nd place · Anthropic's Claude for Public Benefit track · Cal Hacks 13.0

Smart-glasses job coaching for people with intellectual and developmental disabilities · three-person team · 36 hours · Oct 2026

Turns one narrated demonstration into a step-by-step task spec with Claude, then checks each step through the camera and coaches in the supervisor's own voice.

ClaudeCartesiaSpacetimeDBFactory Droid
Team and credit
Built by a three-person team in 36 hours at Cal Hacks 13.0. Everything below is the team's work.
What we built
  • Combines demonstration keyframes (where the hands pause) with a word-level transcript into a structured task specification, using Claude.
  • Live coaching: confidence-gated vision checks against the camera, and coaching audio cached per line in a cloned supervisor voice. Prompts fade as the worker gains mastery.
  • When a step keeps failing, a sandboxed remediation agent rewrites it.
Decision and trade-off
A rewritten step never goes live on its own: the supervisor reviews the before-and-after and approves it. Updates are slower, but a worker never receives an unreviewed instruction.
Evidence
Devpost write-up and the case study on this site. The simulation on the card is a scripted re-creation, not the real system.
Not done yet
It's a 36-hour hackathon prototype. It hasn't been tested with workers on a real job.

Mend

Finalist · Top 15 teams · USC Origin Hackathon 2026

Incentives for street-condition reporting · five-person team · Sep 2026

Built location-based rewards for a five-person hackathon team. Reward multipliers increased in areas with sparse or outdated reports and decreased as coverage improved.

TypeScriptDenoSupabase Edge FunctionsPostgreSQL / PostGISH3
My contribution
The reward calculation, itemized receipts, bounty budgets, and sponsored check-in flows. Each H3 hexagon (about a city block) gets a live 1×–5× multiplier; the rate card is severity × asset tier × first finder × zone, with a daily cap.
Decision and trade-off
One shared pricing module prices both the heat map and the payout, and each report is priced before it's saved. The number a player sees on the map is the number they're paid, and a report can't cool its own cell. The cost: every pricing change touches both surfaces at once.
Also built
  • Bounty points budgets: a bounty stops heating the map once its budget is spent, so spend stays predictable (100 points = $1).
  • Sponsored campaigns: a brand pays per verified store visit. The player files a real report within 300 m, checks in within 75 m, and gets a one-time code the store redeems.
  • Built on top of teammates' gift-card redemption, danger-zone and first-finder rules without changing them.
Evidence
11 Deno unit tests for pricing and campaigns, plus 39 end-to-end checks that run every database migration and the demo seed in PGlite with PostGIS. Code is on GitHub; the Devpost covers the team's full build.
Team context
The five of us shipped an iOS app (SwiftUI) with on-device damage models, server-side vision verification and a city work-queue dashboard, backed by a 51-person survey and an analysis of 1.77M LA 311 requests.
Not done yet
A hackathon build running on seeded demo data. No real payouts or live city usage.

Yet Another Kafka

Open source

A pub-sub message broker built from raw TCP sockets, no frameworks · 2023

20+ topics, 80+ partitions and persistent offset tracking in Java; handled 250+ concurrent clients at 35,000+ messages/sec with sub-50 ms p95 latency.

JavaTCP socketsMultithreading
What it is
A Kafka-style broker in Java: topics split into partitions, producers and consumers over raw TCP sockets, and consumer offsets persisted so a client can resume where it stopped. Bottlenecks were profiled and debugged along the way.
How the numbers were measured
The benchmark conditions (hardware, message size, persistence settings, run length) aren't documented on this site yet. Until they are, read 35,000+ messages/sec and sub-50 ms p95 as results from my own test runs, not a reproducible benchmark.
Evidence
Source code on GitHub. The throughput playground on the card is a simulation of the idea, not the broker itself.
